## Reading the table

- "Participants in BIX files" includes people who were not examined (pregnancy, metal, weight, other reasons). Only
  completed exams (BIAEXSTS = 1) are fitted.
- Every completed exam is refitted, including CDC's 305 "could not be fit" spectra. In 12 spectra (10 coded 5,
  2 coded 4) the resistance at 1 MHz is higher than at 5 kHz, which a Cole spectrum cannot do; their fits are poor,
  as expected.
- The differences are |ours − CDC| / CDC for Re, Ri and fc, and |ours − CDC| for alpha. "our rms" is the median rms
  of the relative complex residual over the 50 frequencies (0.0014 = 0.14%).
- CDC's fc is reported in whole kHz, which alone allows about 1% disagreement at 50 kHz.

## How it could still be wrong

- CDC's fitting method (Xitron's software) is not documented in the files. A different weighting would move Ri and
  alpha most, because they are set by the high-frequency end.
- The labelled frequencies are taken as true. If the device used an exact log grid and CDC rounded the labels, the
  fits that use the labels would be slightly biased. They fit better, which argues against that.
