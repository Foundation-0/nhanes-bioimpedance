## What this says

- **O1.** The 24 spectra CDC could not fit, but that fit well here, mostly have ordinary parameters: Re, fc and Td sit
  inside the 5th–95th percentile range of CDC's "excellent" spectra for 17–20 of them, Ri and alpha for 13. They look
  like usable measurements that CDC's software rejected. A negative time delay marks bad spectra: 36 of the 46 are in
  CDC's "questionable" or "bad" fits, and the refit's residual for them is 27 times the median.
- **O2.** A 50 kHz equation (sex, age, weight, height²/R50) already predicts DXA lean mass with R² ≈ 0.96 on a
  held-out cycle. The full spectrum (height²/Re, height²/Ri, alpha, fc, phase angle at 5 and 200 kHz) adds 0.000 to
  0.002 to R² and lowers the error by up to 0.07 kg. Real but negligible.
- **O3.** In adults aged 20–49 at the exam, 50 kHz phase angle predicts death (hazard ratio 0.66 per degree, adjusted for
  age and sex). The full spectrum does not add consistently: the held-out C-index changes by +0.016, +0.001 and −0.008
  in the three test cycles, and only the first interval excludes zero. With 63–113 deaths per test cycle the
  comparison is weak, and it covers only adults under 50.

## How it could still be wrong

- **Survey design.** It enters through the weights and the PSU-within-strata bootstrap of the test cycle. It is not a
  full design-based variance for the Cox coefficients, so the hazard ratio has no interval here.
- **Same feature set throughout.** Features were chosen before looking at outcomes and not tuned. A different set (for
  example the refit residual, or Td) was not tried, to avoid testing many models on 286 deaths.
- **Imputations.** DXA values are averaged over CDC's five imputations for the bootstrap; R² is averaged over
  imputations, not pooled by Rubin's rules.
- **Exclusions.** Participants with a refit residual above 2% (poor spectra) are left out of O2 and O3.
