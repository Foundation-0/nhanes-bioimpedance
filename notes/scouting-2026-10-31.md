# Scouting notes, 31 October 2026

This is the record of the work done before the repository was set up. It comes from Foundation0's scouting of tissue
electrical properties on 31 October 2026
([report](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/blob/main/deep-fields/scouting/tissue-electrical.md), lead 2) and a
pilot run the same day.

## The question

What do the full 50-frequency bioimpedance spectra measured in NHANES 1999–2004 show beyond the single 50 kHz number
that published studies use?

## Why it was chosen

- **Public, large, and linked.** About 13,300 people aged 8–49, measured with one device and one protocol. The data
  have DXA body composition from the same exam, and adults are linked to death records through the NCHS Linked
  Mortality Files.
- **Under-used in the way that matters.** The raw resistance and reactance at all 50 frequencies are published, but
  the papers found use 50 kHz phase angle, two-frequency ratios, or CDC's fitted Cole parameters.
- **Checkable.** Anyone can download the same files and re-run the fits. An outcome analysis can hold out one survey
  cycle.

## Checked on 31 Oct 2026

- **Device and variables.** The [CDC documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/1999/DataFiles/BIX.htm)
  names the HYDRA ECF/ICF Bio-Impedance Spectrum Analyzer (Model 4200, Xitron Technologies). It lists resistance
  BIXS005K–BIXS1M and reactance BIXC005K–BIXC1M; Cole outputs BIDRECF, BIDRICF, BIDCM, BIDTD, BIDALPHA and BIDFC; a
  fit code BIDFIT (0 excellent to 5 could not be fit, 7 no BIA data); and derived ECF, ICF, TBW, FFM and fat.
- **Exclusions** (1999–2000 documentation): pregnancy, amputations other than fingers or toes, metal implants,
  pacemaker or defibrillator, coronary stents, weight over 300 lb.
- **Counts**, from the files: completed exams 4,269 (1999–2000), 4,709 (2001–02) and 4,298 (2003–04), 13,276 in all.
  Every completed exam has all 100 values.
- **Frequencies.** CDC's documentation says the 50 frequencies are logarithmically spaced. The variable labels give
  5, 6, 7 … 16 kHz in 1 kHz steps, then values close to a log grid, but with round numbers at 50, 100, 200 and
  500 kHz (an exact log grid has 48.4, 103.2, 197.5 and 522.7 kHz). Fitting all spectra with each grid, the labelled
  grid gives the lower residual in 85% of spectra and reproduces CDC's parameters more closely. So the labels are
  taken as the true frequencies.

## From the literature survey, not yet re-checked

- Six papers found by a PubMed search use these files: Kuchnia 2016 (phase angle and 200/5 kHz impedance-ratio
  cut-points, n = 6,237); Stagi 2022 and Marini 2024 (bioimpedance vector analysis); Johnson 2017 (Chamney
  body-composition model); Xia 2025 (50 kHz phase angle vs mortality, 6,338 adults, 307 deaths, hazard ratio 0.73);
  Lafontant 2026 (reference values for Cm and fc). Links are in the
  [survey](reports/literature-survey-2026-10-31.md), section 4.
- UK Biobank measured whole-body impedance in 492,557 participants with a Tanita BC418MA, under controlled access.
  The measurement frequency is not stated in the document the survey read.

## Pilot, 31 Oct 2026

The pilot refits every spectrum with the Hydra 4200 Cole model, including its time-delay term, and compares with
CDC's parameters. The output is in [../results/pilot-2026-10-31.md](../results/pilot-2026-10-31.md).

## Open points

- The Cole fit CDC used (Xitron's software) is not documented in the files. Our fit minimises the relative complex
  residual. Part of the remaining difference in Ri (median 2.3%) may come from that choice.
- A negative time delay, as in 46 of CDC's fits, has no obvious physical meaning.
- The linked mortality files and the survey-design variables are not used yet.
